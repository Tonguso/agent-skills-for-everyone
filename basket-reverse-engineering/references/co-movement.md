# Soft co-movement and cluster evidence

## Purpose
Use a light diagnostic when price relationships could support or distinguish organising
ideas. Discover candidate groups, challenge proposed themes and identify outliers. This
is not a mandatory pass/fail test, a membership filter, or proof of the author's intent.
Weak co-movement does not automatically refute genuine business exposure. Low aggregate
correlation does not refute a multi-theme basket.

## Data and controls
Use actual corporate-action-adjusted returns. State the observation window, frequency,
usable sample size, coverage and appropriate market/sector benchmarks. Distinguish price
adjustment from excess returns or regression residuals; describe the adjustment actually
used. Start with a market-adjusted view where feasible. A sector-adjusted view can help
separate narrower drivers, but may also remove part of the economic theme itself.

Align currencies and trading dates. Flag short histories, stale prices and non-synchronous
market closes where material. Do not silently fill missing prices to create observations.
Use comparable observation sets for comparisons; disclose pairwise sample differences.
For historical reconstruction, use windows available at the construction/rebalance date.
Later windows describe subsequent behaviour, not what the author knew at construction.
Do not infer position signs or weights from returns. If positions are signed, distinguish
company-return relationships from position-level contributions.

## Candidate clusters
1. Inspect the pairwise correlation matrix, using a heatmap or simple hierarchical
   ordering when useful. A rendered chart is optional, not a default new deliverable.
   Examine both business-led proposed groups and price-led candidate groups. Do not force
   a fixed number of clusters, optimise thresholds until the desired groups appear, or
   invent a sub-theme for each isolated name. Record method/settings if clustering is used.
2. Compare within-group with between-group correlations and identify loosely connected
   names or outliers. An overall mean can hide coherent groups with weak links between
   them. Report group sizes and comparable windows; very small groups give weak evidence.
   Preserve overlapping business exposures even if the clustering assigns one group per
   name. Negative relationships may be economically coherent, especially for opposing
   exposures or long/short structures; correlation is not a universal coherence score.
3. Where feasible, check candidate groups in another stated window, preferably a
   non-overlapping window not used to discover them. Report membership instability and
   sign changes. A second window is a sensitivity check, not proof of persistence;
   overlapping windows are not independent validation. If histories are insufficient,
   leave stability untested rather than manufacture observations.
4. Interpret the groups against independently sourced business exposure and shared
   catalysts. Price clusters show shared trading behaviour, not necessarily shared
   themes. Market beta, sector, rates, geography, currency, liquidity or speculative
   sentiment may explain them. Name a thematic cluster only when economic evidence
   supports that label; otherwise describe it as an unexplained price cluster. Do not
   retroactively treat a discovered cluster as a confirmed selection rule.

## Inexpensive random-basket context
When a usable comparison universe and prices are already available or inexpensive to
obtain, try a random same-size basket comparison even if it will not change the thematic
interpretation. Use the same return treatment, windows, coverage rules and cohesion
statistic. State the universe, draw count, sampling method/seed and any matching for
market, sector, size or liquidity. Match to the hypothesis being tested; do not hide
sector matching that conditions away a broad sector theme. Remove duplicate share lines
of the same company from draws unless the comparison explicitly calls for them.

Report the observed mean pairwise correlation, random-basket median, descriptive
percentile and relevant limitations. This contextualises overall cohesion; it does not
identify the economic meaning of clusters. It is not a significance test or evidence of
a narrow theme by itself. A whole-basket baseline cannot validate clusters selected from
the same data. Any group-specific baseline must acknowledge data-driven selection and
small samples. Avoid an exhaustive search or costly price acquisition just to complete
this optional check. If unavailable, state the missing universe/data or cost constraint.

## Output and interpretation
Keep the main answer compact: candidate groups and decisive outliers, within-versus-
between relationships, stability finding, and random-baseline result where run. Retain
methods, dates and coverage in working evidence. Use no fixed correlation cutoff or
mandatory cluster count. Keep observed behaviour separate from inferred economic drivers
and author intent. If skipped or unavailable, state why and which conclusion remains
untested. Do not claim an empirical check was run merely because this procedure exists.
